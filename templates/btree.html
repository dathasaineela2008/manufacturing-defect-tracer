Why B-Trees in Production Systems?

1. Minimizing Disk I/O: In secondary memory storage, disk seeks are thousands of times slower than RAM. A binary tree of 1,000,000 records has a height $\approx 20$ (20 disk seeks). A B-Tree with $t=100$ has a height $\le 3$ (only 3 disk seeks!).

2. Self-Balancing Guarantee: All leaves remain at identical depth. Splitting happens from the bottom up on insert, guaranteeing $O(\log n)$ worst-case bounds.

3. Range Queries: In-order traversal naturally yields sorted keys without costly post-sorting.

Complexity Formula:
Height: $h \le \log_t \frac{n+1}{2}$
Search time: $O(t \cdot \log_t n)$
